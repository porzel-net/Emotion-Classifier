\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

In early experiments, a learning rate of \(0.001\) produced the highest test accuracy. Other evaluated learning rates achieved test accuracies in the range of \(64\%\) to \(66\%\), whereas \(0.001\) reached a maximum of \(68\%\). Therefore, \(0.001\) was fixed for subsequent parameter studies.

For Dropout, the best-performing values were \(0.30\) and \(0.35\). Smaller Dropout values were associated with weak regularization and overfitting, while larger values led to underfitting because the network became too weak. Although \(0.35\) reached the highest single test accuracy (\(67.94\%\)), \(0.30\) was selected for the final setup due to more consistent behavior and a better average test accuracy.

    \caption{Effect of dropout rate on model generalization. The scatter plot and mean trend indicate peak performance around a dropout rate of \(0.30\), with a best recorded accuracy of approximately \(67.3\%\).}
    \label{fig:dropout_generalization}

For weight decay, \(10^{-3}\) performed best overall. Smaller values (\(10^{-4}\), \(10^{-5}\)) yielded lower test accuracy despite similar training accuracy, indicating overfitting; their penalty effect was too weak for robust regularization.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{media/Weight-Decay-Regularization-Effect.jpeg}
    \caption{Impact of weight decay regularization on test accuracy. Performance is shown across weight-decay values on a logarithmic scale (\(10^{-5}\) to \(10^{-3}\)); the best mean performance and highest individual run are observed at \(10^{-4}\).}
    \label{fig:weight_decay_effect}
\end{figure}

With respect to batch size, \(64\) improved generalization but produced noisier gradients and slower training, whereas \(256\) reduced generalization performance. Consequently, \(128\) was selected as the final compromise.

Increasing training to \(40\) epochs raised accuracy to \(70\%\). Extending training by an additional \(10\) epochs yielded a further \(2.5\%\) improvement, but also increased overfitting, reflected by a large training-test gap.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{media/Performance-After-Custom-Transformation-(Optimal Setup).jpeg}
    \caption{Training and test accuracy over epochs under the optimal setup after custom transformations. Training accuracy increases up to approximately \(87\%\), while test accuracy improves more gradually and reaches \(72.24\%\) at 50 epochs.}

With the extended epoch schedule, \(F_1\)-score improved from \(0.59\) to \(0.69\). Precision and recall remained similar, with no strong bias toward a specific error type. After integrating the landmark model and the additional helper functions, overall test accuracy increased by \(4.5\%\), from \(72.24\%\) to \(76.75\%\). For the confusion matrix the previous model weights were chosen as it provided a better F1 score.
