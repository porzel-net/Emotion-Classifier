\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\textit{Metric focus.}
Training was performed over multiple epochs, because facial-expression patterns were not learned reliably in a single pass through the data. Model parameters were updated after each batch via backpropagation. In addition, a validation split was used to estimate generalization to unseen samples, which is critical in facial emotion recognition because models can learn dataset-specific patterns instead of expression-related features and thereby overfit. After each epoch, training accuracy and training loss were computed by comparing predicted and true labels.

Adam was selected as the primary optimizer due to its adaptive update mechanism and broad use in deep-learning practice. Initial tuning experiments evaluated learning rates between \(0.0003\) and \(0.003\), with repeated runs over multiple epochs.

To reduce overfitting, two regularization strategies were applied: Dropout and \(L_2\) weight decay. Dropout was used to reduce reliance on individual neurons, and \(L_2\) weight decay was introduced after overfitting was observed in early tests.

To identify a suitable trade-off between underfitting and overfitting, combinations of regularization settings and epoch counts were evaluated. More than \(15\) parameter combinations were tested repeatedly and averaged. Dropout rates in the range \(0.1\) to \(0.5\) were examined. For \(L_2\) regularization, weight decay values of \(10^{-3}\), \(10^{-4}\), and \(10^{-5}\) were compared.

Epoch counts were also varied. An initial configuration of \(10\) epochs showed underfitting. Further tests were conducted at \(20\) and \(30\) epochs, while runs above \(30\) epochs reached training accuracies above \(99\%\), indicating overfitting. Batch size was additionally evaluated at \(64\), \(128\), and \(256\).

After introducing a custom transformation, \(30\) epochs were no longer sufficient, and additional epochs were required to avoid underfitting. Loss decreased more slowly with the custom transformation, but reached a comparable level after longer training, which motivated extending the epoch count. At this stage, evaluation functions for precision, recall, and \(F_1\)-score were also integrated.

Furthermore, a landmark model and a new dataset were introduced. After renewed overfitting was observed, additional functions were implemented to improve test accuracy: a Sobel feature branch (separate CNN path for edge information) and width-multiplier support to simplify experiments with different image resolutions, which also influences regularization behavior implicitly.

In addition to scalar metrics (Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and \(F_1\)), we use a confusion matrix for class-wise error analysis, so misclassification structure can be inspected beyond aggregate scores.
